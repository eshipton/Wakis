# Chapter 7 · Light Sources

Spontaneous, partially coherent, and coherent light emitters—planar deep-UV LEDs, superluminescent diodes, microLED collimators, RGB microLED arrays, partially coherent interferometry, Helmholtz-reciprocity emission-as-absorption modeling, and bound-state-in-the-continuum (BIC) lasers—simulated with the Finite Integration Technique (FIT) in Wakis.

Chapter 7 builds on [Tutorial 4.3 · Dipole near a mirror: image theory, Drexhage effect and Purcell factor](../04_radiation_and_antennas/04-03_dipole_near_mirror/dipole_near_mirror.ipynb) as the basic point-dipole emitter model in a stratified or reflective environment.

## Tutorials

- **[Tutorial 7.1 · Planar deep-UV LED: escape cone, total internal reflection, TE vs TM dipoles](07-01_planar_led/planar_led.ipynb)** ([`planar_led.py`](07-01_planar_led/planar_led.py)) — Planar $\text{AlGaN}$ deep-UV LED ($\lambda_0 = 280\;\text{nm}$, Ryu *et al.* 2014): critical-angle escape cone, waveguide trapping by total internal reflection, TE ($E_\parallel$) vs TM ($E_\perp$) quantum-well dipole light-extraction efficiency across absorbing $\text{p-GaN}$ thickness, and multi-dipole incoherent ensemble averaging.
- **[Tutorial 7.2 · Superluminescent diode: residual reflectivity of a tilted facet](07-02_sled_tilted_facet/sled_tilted_facet.ipynb)** ([`sled_tilted_facet.py`](07-02_sled_tilted_facet/sled_tilted_facet.py)) — Modal retro-reflection $R_{00}(\theta_{\text{tilt}})$ of a tilted semiconductor waveguide facet against Marcuse (1989) overlap theory, Fabry–Pérot spectral ripple suppression, quarter-wave $\text{Si}_3\text{N}_4$ anti-reflection coating ($81\times$ reflectivity reduction), and Snell refraction in the far field.

- **[Tutorial 7.5 · Partially coherent light through a double slit](07-05_partial_coherence_double_slit/partial_coherence_double_slit.ipynb)** ([`partial_coherence_double_slit.py`](07-05_partial_coherence_double_slit/partial_coherence_double_slit.py)) — Young's double slit illuminated by spatially and temporally partially coherent light: van Cittert–Zernike theorem ($\mu_{12} = \operatorname{sinc}(D a / \lambda_0 L_1)$, fringe visibility $V = |\mu_{12}|$, and secondary-lobe phase reversal), Gaussian Schell-model coherent-mode decomposition, broadband white-light envelope, and Drude silver vs PEC screen transmission.
- **[Tutorial 7.6 · Thin-film LED: emission computed as absorption (Helmholtz reciprocity)](07-06_led_reciprocity/led_reciprocity.ipynb)** ([`led_reciprocity.py`](07-06_led_reciprocity/led_reciprocity.py)) — Treating an incoherent thin-film LED as an absorber via Helmholtz reciprocity ($0.07\%$ agreement with direct dipole summation) and optimizing grating extraction (Janssen *et al.* 2010: shallow-grating $Q \sim 10^5$ PEC vs $10^3$ Ag resonances, $(t, d)$ landscape with steepest-ascent paths and adjoint/FD gradients, optimal active-layer position near $120\;\text{nm}$, pitch scan peaking near $\Lambda/\lambda_0 \approx 0.8$, and $\approx 2\times$ enhancement over the optimized flat structure).
- **[Tutorial 7.7 · DFB laser with a quarter-wave phase shift](07-07_dfb_laser/dfb_laser.ipynb)** ([`dfb_laser.py`](07-07_dfb_laser/dfb_laser.py)) — Index-coupled distributed feedback (DFB) laser against Kogelnik & Shank (1972) and Haus & Shank (1976) coupled-wave theory: uniform stopband-edge two-mode degeneracy vs $\lambda/4$-shifted single Bragg defect mode ($\delta_0 L = 0$), 1D and 2D $\text{TE}_0$ slab-waveguide linear-gain `harminv` threshold extraction across $\kappa L \in [0.5, 4.0]$, $O(N_\Lambda^{-2})$ grid convergence, above-threshold `SaturableGain` single-mode clamping ($\text{SMSR} = 40\;\text{dB}$), spatial hole burning, and coupled-wave Rigrod L–I slope efficiency.
- **[Tutorial 7.8 · Microdisk whispering-gallery laser](07-08_microdisk_laser/microdisk_laser.ipynb)** ([`microdisk_laser.py`](07-08_microdisk_laser/microdisk_laser.py)) — Dielectric microdisk whispering-gallery laser ($R = 1.0\;\mu\text{m}$, $n_{\text{disk}} = 2.0$, Chang & Taflove 2004): exact Bessel/Hankel complex roots for cold-cavity frequencies and radiative $Q_{\text{rad}}(m)$ exponential growth, subpixel vs staircase boundary scattering, linear Lorentz gain threshold $g_{\text{th}} = n_{\text{disk}}\omega/(c\Gamma Q_{\text{cold}})$ ($0.14\%$ mean agreement across $m = 4\text{--}8$), active `FourLevelGain` single-mode lasing build-up, annular ring-pump radial mode selection, $L$–pump threshold curve with 2D spatial hole burning, and bus-waveguide output coupling.
- **[Tutorial 7.9 · Lasing from a bound state in the continuum](07-09_bic_laser/bic_laser.ipynb)** ([`bic_laser.py`](07-09_bic_laser/bic_laser.py)) — Symmetry-protected vs quasi-BICs across dimer bar widths $w_0 \in [140, 200]\;\text{nm}$ in 2D FIT (Kodigala *et al.* 2017; Hsu *et al.* 2016), Bloch momentum scaling $Q(k_\parallel) \propto k_\parallel^{-2}$, 3D far-field polarization vortex on an off-mirror loop ($q = \pm 1$), finite $N$-period strip scaling $Q_1(N) \propto N^p$ vs fixed-$N$ envelope order $Q_m(N) \approx Q_1(N)/m^2$, single-mode lasing threshold and spatial-hole-burning L–I curves with `wakis.gain` (`SaturableGain` and noise-seeded `FourLevelGain`), `Near2FarMonitor` doughnut far-field beam, and direct finite-strip $\mathcal{C}_2$ symmetry-breaking mutation ($\alpha \ne 0$) raising threshold as $\alpha^2$.
- **[Tutorial 7.10 · Photonic-crystal surface-emitting laser (PCSEL): band-edge modes and 3D coupled-wave theory](07-10_pcsel/pcsel.ipynb)** ([`pcsel.py`](07-10_pcsel/pcsel.py)) — Square-lattice $\text{GaAs/InGaAs}$ PCSEL ($a = 290\;\text{nm}$, Liang *et al.* 2011, 2012; Yoshida *et al.* 2019, 2023): 3D FIT Bloch unit-cell $\Gamma$-point band-edge modes A–D (symmetry-protected $A_2/B_2$ BICs vs $E$ radiative doublet), near-$\Gamma$ $\Gamma\text{--}X/\Gamma\text{--}M$ bands, `wakis.pcsel_cwt` 3D coupled-wave theory validation and large-fill-factor vertical mode expulsion breakdown, and finite-size $L\times L$ threshold-gain margins of the double lattice (Hermitian/non-Hermitian coupling cancellation) against matched and circular-hole single lattices, including where the double lattice loses its advantage.
