# Chapter 9 · Inverse Design

Gradient-based electromagnetic topology and shape optimization using Wakis's exact discrete adjoint solver (`wakis.inverse`) and JAX integration (`jax_simulation`).

Adjoint sensitivity analysis computes the gradient of a scalar figure of merit with respect to thousands or millions of permittivity parameters in two simulations of Maxwell's equations—one forward run and one backward (transpose) run—independently of the parameter count ([Giles & Pierce 2000, adjoint approach to design](https://doi.org/10.1023/A:1011430410075); [Molesky et al. 2018, inverse design in nanophotonics](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41566-018-0246-9); [Hughes et al. 2019, forward-mode differentiation](https://doi.org/10.1021/acsphotonics.9b01238)). Much of the foundational formulation, device demonstrations, benchmark suites, and physical performance bounds comes from Owen D. Miller's group at Yale ([millergroup.yale.edu](https://millergroup.yale.edu)): deriving electromagnetic shape and topology derivatives from fundamental solar-cell and wave physics ([O. D. Miller, *Photonic Design: From Fundamental Solar Cell Physics to Computational Inverse Design*, PhD thesis, UC Berkeley, 2012, arXiv:1308.0212](https://arxiv.org/abs/1308.0212)) and summarizing the general inverse optical design framework ([O. D. Miller & E. Yablonovitch, "Inverse Optical Design", *Encyclopedia of Applied and Computational Mathematics*, pp. 729–732, Springer, 2015](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-540-70529-1_45)); demonstrating level-set and boundary-perturbation adjoint shape optimization applied to electromagnetic design of compact waveguide splitters and couplers ([C. M. Lalau-Keraly, S. Bhargava, O. D. Miller & E. Yablonovitch, *Opt. Express* **21**, 21693, 2013](https://doi.org/10.1364/OE.21.021693)); extending topology optimization to broadband high-NA achromatic metalenses by inverse design ([H. Chung & O. D. Miller, *Opt. Express* **28**, 6945, 2020](https://doi.org/10.1364/OE.385440)); establishing computational bounds to light–matter interactions via local conservation laws ([Z. Kuang & O. D. Miller, *Phys. Rev. Lett.* **125**, 263607, 2020](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.125.263607)); and co-developing the community validation and characterization of algorithms and software for photonics inverse design ([M. Chen et al., *J. Opt. Soc. Am. B* **41**, A161, 2024](https://doi.org/10.1364/JOSAB.506412)) from which the `invrs-gym` ([M. F. Schubert et al., arXiv:2410.24132, 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.24132)) metalens and metagrating challenges originate.

## Tutorials

- **[9.1 Adjoint gradients](09-01_adjoint_gradients/adjoint_gradients.ipynb)** ([`09-01_adjoint_gradients`](09-01_adjoint_gradients/adjoint_gradients.py)) — Exact discrete transpose of the leapfrog FIT time-stepper (`AdjointSimulation`, `AdjointStepper`), why discretize-then-differentiate is exact to round-off vs continuous-adjoint mismatch, `MaterialGrid` density parameterization with conic filtering, $\tanh$ projection ($\beta$-continuation) and subpixel-smoothed tensor averaging (`smoothing="tensor"`), operator dot-product and central finite-difference V-curve verification, the four-rung memory ladder (`store`, Griewank–Walther `checkpoint`, CPML-interface `reverse`, and `nyquist` following [Park, Miller & Chung 2026, arXiv:2607.08159](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08159)), and a live 2D waveguide-bend topology optimization.
- **[9.2 Waveguide bend](09-02_waveguide_bend/waveguide_bend.ipynb)** ([`09-02_waveguide_bend`](09-02_waveguide_bend/waveguide_bend.py)) — Topology optimization of the `invrs-gym` `ceviche_waveguide_bend` challenge (10 nm grid, $312\times 312$ pixels, $160\times 160$ design region, 6 wavelengths, 8 px minimum feature length scale), two-stage `FeasibleDensity` L-BFGS-B ($\beta=4..32$) + morphological brush STE ($\beta=64$) pipeline, broadband Wakis re-simulation vs stored `dense_S` and `ceviche` FDFD cross-validation ($\max|\Delta|S|^2| = 3.77\times 10^{-4}$ at $10\text{ nm}$), and like-for-like leaderboard comparison ($+0.6427$ and $+0.5793$ at $8/8\text{ px}$ vs $-1.2504$ at $9/7\text{ px}$).
- **[9.3 Power splitter and mode converter](09-03_splitter_and_mode_converter/splitter_and_mode_converter.ipynb)** ([`09-03_splitter_and_mode_converter`](09-03_splitter_and_mode_converter/splitter_and_mode_converter.py)) — Multi-objective broadband S-parameter optimization of a $1\times 2$ power splitter (`ceviche_power_splitter`, $+0.0682$ at $8/8\text{ px}$ vs $+0.0090$ leaderboard best) and a $\text{TM}_0 \to \text{TM}_1$ waveguide mode converter (`ceviche_mode_converter`, $+0.3122$ at $8/8\text{ px}$), including standalone Wakis re-simulation and diagnostic analysis of the mode-converter gap.
- **[9.4 Metalens](09-04_metalens/metalens.ipynb)** ([`09-04_metalens`](09-04_metalens/metalens.py)) — The `invrs-gym` `metalens` challenge (2D $x\text{--}y$ cross-section, periodic $16\,\mu\text{m}$, focal-intensity enhancement at $450/550/650\text{ nm}$, official `fmmax` evaluator): our Round-2 lens with guaranteed $5/5\text{ px}$ ($100\text{ nm}$) length scale ($8.87\times$ enhancement), the Round-1 unconstrained lens ($14.07\times$ at $2/1\text{ px}$), and the Wakis vs `fmmax` cross-check on the `Rasmus123nm` reference design.
