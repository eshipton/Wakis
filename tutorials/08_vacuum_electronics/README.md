# Chapter 8 · Vacuum Electronics

Slow-wave structures, space-charge waves on drifting electron beams, traveling-wave tubes, backward-wave oscillators, klystrons, and microwave amplifier circuits modeled with the Finite Integration Technique (FIT) in Wakis.

For cold-test dispersion and Pierce interaction impedance of corrugated and glide-symmetric slow-wave structures, see [Tutorial 6.2 · Slow-wave structure cold test: corrugated and glide-symmetric waveguides](../06_periodic_structures/06-02_sws_cold_test/sws_cold_test.ipynb) in Chapter 6.

## Tutorials

- **[Tutorial 8.1 · Space-charge waves on a drifting electron beam](08-01_space_charge_waves/space_charge_waves.ipynb)** ([`space_charge_waves.py`](08-01_space_charge_waves/space_charge_waves.py)) — Fast and slow space-charge waves on a drifting relativistic electron beam (`LinearizedBeam`) inside a conducting drift tube: Doppler-shifted dispersion $\omega = k_z v_0 \pm R\,\omega_p/\gamma_0^{3/2}$, plasma-frequency reduction factor $R(\gamma_0 b, \gamma_0 a)$ in 2D slab and 3D cylindrical geometry, klystron ballistic current-bunching beat $\lambda_q = \pi v_0 / \omega_q$, and Chu's small-signal kinetic power theorem.
- **[Tutorial 8.2 · Cherenkov radiation from a moving charge](08-02_cherenkov_radiation/cherenkov_radiation.ipynb)** ([`cherenkov_radiation.py`](08-02_cherenkov_radiation/cherenkov_radiation.py)) — 3D Cherenkov radiation from a moving Gaussian bunch (`Beam`): Mach cone angle $\cos\theta_c = 1/(\beta n)$ and shock half-angle $90^\circ - \theta_c$, Frank–Tamm spectrum $d^2W/(d\omega\,dz)$ with the coherent bunch form factor $F(\omega)$, sub-threshold contracted Coulomb field ($\beta n < 1$), dispersive Lorentz passband/stopband edge, and elimination of spurious numerical Cherenkov radiation in vacuum ($\beta = 0.999$) using the longitudinal dispersion-free explicit TE/TM scheme (`scheme="tete"`).
- **[Tutorial 8.3 · Laser acceleration of electrons in vacuum — from Lawson–Woodward to attosecond bunches](08-03_vacuum_laser_acceleration/vacuum_laser_acceleration.ipynb)** ([`vacuum_laser_acceleration.py`](08-03_vacuum_laser_acceleration/vacuum_laser_acceleration.py)) — Built up from the Maxwellian picture of Zolotorev, Chattopadhyay and McDonald (the energy an electron gains is minus the interference energy between the drive field and its own radiation), after Zolotorev, Stupakov, Huang, Plettner and colleagues: an electrostatic gap and a 3D FIT pillbox cavity (gain $= qVT\cos\phi$ from interference with the wake); an electron in a plane wave (figure-of-eight orbit, drift velocity, effective mass); a focused beam in 3D FIT (Gouy phase, $v_p > c$, $v_g < c$, the Lawson–Woodward theorem, and why a weak field gives no gain); breaking Lawson–Woodward with a reflecting screen, a two-screen laser cavity and the aperture limit $a \lesssim \gamma\bar\lambda$; ponderomotive acceleration, formation length and the snowplow model with fitted prefactors; the two-mode crater trap and $\approx 50$ as bunches; and the laser as an X-ray FEL wiggler.
- **[Tutorial 8.4 · Smith–Purcell radiation and the dielectric laser accelerator grating](08-04_smith_purcell_dla/smith_purcell_dla.ipynb)** ([`smith_purcell_dla.py`](08-04_smith_purcell_dla/smith_purcell_dla.py)) — Relativistic charge passing over metallic (PEC) and fused-silica gratings (`WakeSolver` / `SolverFIT3D`) on uniform and locally refined interface-snapped graded meshes (`graded_axis`): Smith–Purcell space-harmonic dispersion $\lambda = (d/n)(\beta^{-1} - \cos\theta)$, evanescent impact-parameter decay $\exp(-4\pi h/(\beta\gamma\lambda))$, and the inverse process (dielectric laser acceleration, Peralta *et al.* 2013 & Sapra *et al.* 2020) with synchronous period $\lambda_p = \beta\lambda_0$, wake-integral accelerating gradient vs tooth depth and duty cycle, and flat-surface Lawson–Woodward null.
- **[Tutorial 8.5 · An electron bunch passing through a slow-wave structure: beam–wave impedance](08-05_bunch_through_sws/bunch_through_sws.ipynb)** ([`bunch_through_sws.py`](08-05_bunch_through_sws/bunch_through_sws.py)) — Rigid sub-relativistic electron bunch (`Beam`, `WakeSolver`) crossing finite matched slow-wave structures reused from Tutorial 6.2 (2D G-band Karetnikova sheet-beam grating at $20\;\mathrm{kV}, u_0 = 0.2719\,c$ and 3D V-band Marosi staggered-pillar glide SWS at $5.2\;\mathrm{kV}, u_0 = 0.1416\,c$): Lorentz-reciprocity beam–wave resistance $\operatorname{Re} Z_\parallel(\omega) = \frac{1}{4}\beta_n^2 K_n L^2 \operatorname{sinc}^2(\Delta\beta L / 2)$, quadratic peak scaling $\propto L^2$ and group-velocity walk-off linewidth $\propto (L|1/u_0 - 1/v_g|)^{-1}$, independent extraction of Pierce $K_n$ and sheet-beam averaging, infinite-structure stopping power $\mathrm{d}W/\mathrm{d}z$, backward- vs forward-wave Poynting flux direction, and shorted-cavity standing-wave $(R/Q)_m$ relation to $K_n$.
- **[Tutorial 8.6 · Traveling-wave-tube small-signal gain: a linearized electron beam in a slow-wave structure](08-06_twt_small_signal_gain/twt_small_signal_gain.ipynb)** ([`twt_small_signal_gain.py`](08-06_twt_small_signal_gain/twt_small_signal_gain.py)) — Small-signal TWT gain from Pierce 3-wave and 4-wave theory and from time-domain 2D/3D FIT hot tests (`SolverFIT3D` + `LinearizedBeam`) on the Karetnikova $0.2\;\mathrm{THz}$ staggered double-vane grating and the Marosi V-band glide-symmetric SWS, with the cold parameters ($\beta_c$, $K$, loss) measured on each FIT grid: growth rate vs current, detuning and space charge, real code-path mutations, the paper's reported gains compared line by line, and an honest account of where FIT and Pierce disagree (pre-asymptotic convergence, a mirror asymmetry not predicted by Pierce theory, a 3D level that is not converged).
- **[Tutorial 8.7 · A metamaterial slow-wave structure: reversed Cherenkov radiation](08-07_metamaterial_sws_reversed_cherenkov/metamaterial_sws.ipynb)** ([`metamaterial_sws.py`](08-07_metamaterial_sws_reversed_cherenkov/metamaterial_sws.py)) — All-metal complementary electric split-ring resonator (CeSRR) slow-wave structure in a below-cutoff square waveguide (Duan *et al.*, "Observation of the reversed Cherenkov radiation," *Nat.
