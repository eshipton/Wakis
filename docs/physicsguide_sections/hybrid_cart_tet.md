### 🧩 Hybrid Cartesian / tetrahedral FIT: `wakis.hybrid` (milestone 1)

`wakis.hybrid.HybridFIT3D` keeps a Cartesian FIT grid everywhere and replaces an axis-aligned box of whole Cartesian cells by a tetrahedral FIT mesh, so that curved or small geometry is meshed with tetrahedra only where it is. The two grids are coupled through the tangential trace of the hexahedral Nédélec element on the box surface, using the same hanging-edge construction as the 3D subgrids (`wakis.subgrid3d`, [subgrid3d.md](subgrid3d.md)). The result is one discrete Maxwell system with symmetric positive-definite (SPD) mass matrices. Energy conservation, the stability bound and the exact transpose of a step therefore all follow from the algebra and do not depend on the mesh. Milestone 1 is a CPU (NumPy/SciPy) prototype: one box patch, vacuum or per-tag isotropic $\varepsilon,\mu$ and PEC obstacles in the tetrahedra, per-cell $\varepsilon,\mu$ and PEC cells in the Cartesian grid, PEC/PMC outer walls, soft edge currents and probes, and a frequency-domain solve with complex coordinate stretching.

#### 1. The options for the interface, and the choice

The Cartesian side is the lowest-order hexahedral edge element with trapezoidal (row-sum) mass lumping, which is exactly FIT/Yee. Its tangential trace on a cell face is the bilinear Nédélec field of the four face edges. The tetrahedral side is Whitney 1-forms, with triangular faces. Three ways to make the two meet:

| Option | Idea | For | Against |
|---|---|---|---|
| (a) Pyramid transition layer (Rylander & Bondeson 2000, 2002) | one layer of pyramids whose square bases match the hexahedra and whose triangles match the tetrahedra; one global FEM with mass lumping in the hexahedra | conforming in $H(\mathrm{curl})$; proven stable FEM–FDTD hybrid | needs pyramid edge elements (rational basis functions) and their mass; a structured layer must be generated; Rylander & Bondeson step the FEM part implicitly (Newmark) |
| (b) Free diagonal edges | the diagonals of the split squares become unknowns; the outer hexahedron becomes a 7-faced polyhedron | no constraint | the outer cell needs a non-diagonal, non-orthogonal Hodge for the diagonal edge; the FIT structure of the Cartesian side is lost |
| (c) Hanging tetrahedral surface edges **(chosen)** | every tetrahedral edge on the box surface is the line integral of the Cartesian trace of its square | the Cartesian grid is untouched; one sparse prolongation $\mathbf P$; the subgrid machinery (energy, transpose, Lanczos) carries over unchanged; the tetrahedral surface may refine the squares arbitrarily | the tetrahedral tangential field on the interface is restricted to the Cartesian trace space (the patch surface cannot carry finer tangential detail than the Cartesian squares); the coupling is weak (de Rham-consistent), not $H(\mathrm{curl})$-conforming |

Edelvik & Ledfelt (2000) build an explicit FDTD–FEM hybrid on the same "FDTD = lumped hexahedral FEM" observation, with a hexahedral overlap region in the FEM grid. Option (c) keeps a single mesh with no overlap.

#### 2. The discrete system

**Unknowns.** Cartesian edge voltages and face fluxes of the active cells, i.e. the cells outside the box (`CartesianBlock` of `wakis.subgrid3d`), followed by tetrahedral edge voltages and face fluxes (`SimplexMesh`). The Cartesian edges on the box surface are free, and they are the masters of the interface. The tetrahedral edges on the box surface are hanging. Cartesian edges strictly inside the box belong to no active cell and are dead. Tetrahedral edges on other boundary faces (holes) and on PEC tetrahedra are fixed at zero (PEC).

**The trace.** Let a box face have normal $a$ and tangential axes $(b,c)$, and let the Cartesian square be $[u_0,u_1]\times[v_0,v_1]$ with edge voltages $e_{b0}$ (at $v=v_0$), $e_{b1}$, $e_{c0}$ (at $u=u_0$), $e_{c1}$. The tangential Nédélec field is

```math
E_b(v)=\frac{e_{b0}(v_1-v)+e_{b1}(v-v_0)}{h_u h_v},\qquad
E_c(u)=\frac{e_{c0}(u_1-u)+e_{c1}(u-u_0)}{h_u h_v}.
```

Along any straight segment $p\to q$ inside the square, the integrand is linear, so the midpoint rule $(u_m,v_m)$ is exact:

```math
e_{pq}=\frac{\Delta u}{h_u}\Big[(1-t_v)e_{b0}+t_ve_{b1}\Big]+\frac{\Delta v}{h_v}\Big[(1-t_u)e_{c0}+t_ue_{c1}\Big],\quad t_u=\frac{u_m-u_0}{h_u},\ t_v=\frac{v_m-v_0}{h_v}.
```

`box_trace` assembles these rows. A tetrahedral edge that coincides with a Cartesian edge (or with a piece of one) gets weight $\Delta u/h_u$ on that edge alone. A diagonal of a square gets $\tfrac12$ on each of the four edges, with the signs of $\Delta u,\Delta v$. On an edge shared by two squares the two traces agree (the tangential component along a Cartesian edge is $e/h$ in both), so the choice of square does not matter there. The prolongation is

```math
\mathbf e_\text{all}=\mathbf P\mathbf e,\qquad \mathbf P=\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf I\\ \mathbf T\end{bmatrix}\ \text{(free rows / hanging rows; dead and fixed rows are 0)} .
```

**Operators.** Let $\mathbf C=\mathrm{blockdiag}(\mathbf C_\text{cart},\mathbf C_\text{tet})$ be the face-edge incidence of both complexes and $\mathbf K=\mathbf C\mathbf P$. Then

```math
\mathbf W=\mathrm{diag}(\mathbf W_\text{cart})\oplus\mathbf M_2^\text{tet},\qquad
\mathbf M=\mathbf P^T\big(\mathrm{diag}(\mathbf m_\text{cart})\oplus\mathbf M_1^\text{tet}\big)\mathbf P .
```

* $\mathbf W_\text{cart}$ and $\mathbf m_\text{cart}$ are the FIT half-cell / quarter-cell sums over active cells only. A box-surface square therefore sees only its outer half cell, $W=(h_a/2)/(\mu A)$.
* $\mathbf M_1^\text{tet}$ and $\mathbf M_2^\text{tet}$ are the consistent Whitney 1-form and 2-form mass matrices (`whitney_mass_matrices`). A box-surface triangle sees only its inner tetrahedron.
* The interface Cartesian edges collect the mass of their hanging children through the Galerkin product $\mathbf P^T\mathbf M_1\mathbf P$.

Both $\mathbf W$ and $\mathbf M$ are SPD for any non-degenerate mesh, and $\mathbf M$ is SPD because $\mathbf P$ has full column rank. The leapfrog is

```math
\mathbf b^{n+1/2}=\mathbf b^{n-1/2}-\Delta t\,\mathbf K\mathbf e^n,\qquad
\mathbf M(\mathbf e^{n+1}-\mathbf e^n)=\Delta t\big(\mathbf K^T\mathbf W\mathbf b^{n+1/2}-\mathbf P^T\mathbf i^{n+1/2}\big).
```

#### 3. Properties (proved here, tested in `tests/test_105_hybrid_cart_tet.py`)

1. **Discrete de Rham sequence.** Take a nodal potential $\phi$ on the Cartesian nodes and the interior tetrahedral nodes. On the box-surface tetrahedral nodes, let $\phi$ take the bilinear ($Q_1$) interpolant of its square, which is the trace of the trilinear $Q_1$ nodal element whose gradient lies in the hexahedral Nédélec space. Its edge differences then satisfy $\mathbf P\mathbf e_\text{free}=\mathbf G\phi$ exactly, since the line integral of $\nabla Q_1$ along a segment is the difference of the end values. So $\mathbf K\mathbf e=\mathbf C\mathbf G\phi=0$, and the static null space consists of gradients only: no spurious resonances. *Test:* $|\mathbf C\mathbf P\mathbf G\phi|/|\mathbf G\phi|\approx10^{-15}$ on Kuhn and Delaunay patches. The `nearest` and `inject` trace mutations give $0.04$–$0.29$.
2. **Flux bookkeeping.** The trace field has a constant normal curl on each square. Every triangle inside a square therefore receives the area fraction of the square's circulation, and the triangles of a square sum to its circulation exactly. The square flux and the sum of its triangle fluxes (`interface_flux_mismatch`) differ by a constant in time, zero from rest. Every Cartesian cell and every tetrahedron is divergence-free ($\mathbf D\mathbf C=0$ per complex). *Test:* $\le2\times10^{-15}$ relative after a pulse. The `scale` mutation (all trace weights ×1.01) gives $10^{-2}$.
3. **Energy.** For any SPD $\mathbf W$ and $\mathbf M$ the leapfrog conserves
   ```math
   \mathcal E=\tfrac12\,\mathbf b^{n+1/2\,T}\mathbf W\mathbf b^{n+1/2}+\tfrac12\,\mathbf e^{nT}\mathbf M\mathbf e^{n+1}
   ```
   (source-free). $\mathcal E$ is positive iff $\Delta t<2/\sqrt{\lambda_\max(\mathbf M^{-1}\mathbf K^T\mathbf W\mathbf K)}$, which `dt_max` computes by Lanczos. *Test:* relative drift $<10^{-15}$ over 200 steps (LU and Chebyshev mass solves, Kuhn and Delaunay patches). The `asym` mutation, an e-update with a different trace than the b-update, drifts by $>10^{-4}$.
4. **The finer of the two bounds.** Write $\mathbf e_\text{all}=\mathbf P\mathbf e$ and split it into its Cartesian part $\mathbf x_c$ and its tetrahedral part $\mathbf x_t$. The Rayleigh quotient of the composite operator is
   ```math
   \frac{\mathbf x_c^T\mathbf A_c\mathbf x_c+\mathbf x_t^T\mathbf A_t\mathbf x_t}{\mathbf x_c^T\mathbf M_c\mathbf x_c+\mathbf x_t^T\mathbf M_t\mathbf x_t}\le\max\Big(\frac{\mathbf x_c^T\mathbf A_c\mathbf x_c}{\mathbf x_c^T\mathbf M_c\mathbf x_c},\frac{\mathbf x_t^T\mathbf A_t\mathbf x_t}{\mathbf x_t^T\mathbf M_t\mathbf x_t}\Big)\le\max(\lambda_c,\lambda_t),
   ```
   which is the mediant inequality. Here $\lambda_c$ and $\lambda_t$ are the largest eigenvalues of each region alone with its interface edges free (natural boundary), from `block_lambda_bounds`. The hybrid step is therefore never smaller than the smaller of the two regions' own steps: the coupling adds no stiffness of its own. *Test:* $\lambda_\max\le\max(\lambda_c,\lambda_t)$ to $10^{-8}$. In practice the tetrahedral region sets the step (Section 7).
5. **Exact transpose.** `linear_step_T` applies the transposed sequence $(\mathbf M^{-T},\mathbf W^T,\mathbf K^T,\mathbf P)$. With the Chebyshev solve below, $\mathbf M^{-1}$ is replaced by a fixed symmetric polynomial, which is its own transpose. *Test:* dot-product mismatch $\approx10^{-16}$. A non-symmetric perturbation of the mass block, which the symmetric Chebyshev transpose ignores, fails the test, as it must. With LU (exact `trans='T'`) the perturbed system still passes.

#### 4. Solving with the mass matrix

$\mathbf M$ is diagonal on the Cartesian edges away from the patch and coupled on the tetrahedral edges and the interface Cartesian edges. `MassSolver` splits off the coupled rows. Sparse LU of a 3D consistent mass matrix fills badly: $4.7\times10^7$ factor entries for a 26 000-row block. The default for blocks above 4000 rows is therefore a fixed-degree Chebyshev iteration on the Jacobi-scaled block $\mathbf S=\mathbf D^{-1/2}\mathbf B\mathbf D^{-1/2}$:

```math
\mathbf x=\mathbf D^{-1/2}\,p_k(\mathbf S)\,\mathbf D^{-1/2}\mathbf y,\qquad k=\Big\lceil\frac{\ln(2/\text{tol})}{\ln(1/q)}\Big\rceil,\quad q=\frac{\sqrt\kappa-1}{\sqrt\kappa+1}.
```

* Mass matrices are spectrally equivalent to their diagonal, with element-wise bounds (Wathen 1987). The bounds depend only on the worst element shape, not on $h$ (the Kuhn and small-Delaunay condition-number range from wave 2 is not in a JSON in this branch and was not re-measured in wave 3; for tol $=10^{-16}$, `studies/hybrid-cart-tet/results_mixed.json` records `degree = 162` on key `test_delaunay` and `degree = 86` on key `cavity_N16`).
* On the PEC-sphere Delaunay meshes the degree nevertheless **grows with refinement**: 204 at $N=10$, 502 at $N=16$. That means $\kappa$ grew about 6×, because larger meshes contain worse slivers, which set the element-wise bound. Mesh quality, not $h$, controls this cost.
* The spectral bounds come from Lanczos, with factors 0.9 and 1.02.
* $p_k(\mathbf S)$ is a fixed polynomial of a symmetric matrix. The step is exactly linear and its transpose is the same operator. The scheme then conserves the energy of the SPD mass $p_k(\mathbf S)^{-1}$, which differs from $\mathbf M$ by at most tol.
* Measured on the 26 000-row block: residual $9\times10^{-16}$, symmetry error $3\times10^{-16}$, 36 ms per solve on one core.

Lumping the tetrahedral mass instead (the diagonal Voronoi Hodge of `UnstructuredFIT3D`) would make the update fully explicit. On these meshes, however, 1.3–1.7 % of the interior edges have dual areas below $10^{-2}L^2$, and the box-surface edges have negative dual areas, down to $-0.54L^2$, because circumcentres fall outside the box. The Whitney masses are SPD on any mesh.

#### Partial lumping (`mass='mixed'`)

`HybridFIT3D(..., mass="mixed")` (`wakis/hybrid/mass_mixed.py`, tests in `tests/test_109_hybrid_time_domain.py`, benchmark in `studies/hybrid-cart-tet/measure_mixed.py` with output in `studies/hybrid-cart-tet/results_mixed.json` from Batch job `w3hyb-m2` and `studies/hybrid-cart-tet/results_smooth.json` from Batch job `w3hyb-m3`) replaces the consistent Whitney edge mass on a safe interior subset of tetrahedral edges by the diagonal Voronoi (circumcentric) Hodge of `UnstructuredFIT3D` (Clemens & Weiland 2001) while keeping the consistent Whitney block everywhere else. The default `mass="consistent"` path is unchanged bit-for-bit.

1. **Edge partition ($L$ and $C$).** Let $\mathbf M_1$ be the consistent tetrahedral 1-form mass matrix (`whitney_mass_matrices` with the `live` mask applied, SPD on the live tetrahedral edges). `mixed_partition` partitions the $n_{Et}$ tetrahedral edges into $L$ (lumped, `lump_mask`) and $C$ (consistent, `~lump_mask`). An edge $e$ belongs to $L$ if and only if:
   * (a) it does not lie on the box surface (`~on_box`);
   * (b) it is not a hanging edge (`~on_box`) and not a fixed/PEC edge (`~fixed`);
   * (c) every tetrahedron $T$ incident to $e$ is live ($\mathrm{live}_T=1$) and strictly well-centred (`well_centred_tets`, relative `margin` $\delta>0$: its circumcentre $\mathbf c_T$ lies strictly inside $T$ at signed distance $s_{f,T}>\delta h_T$ from all four faces $f\subset T$, where $h_T$ is the mean edge length of $T$, and all six per-tetrahedron dual edge areas satisfy $\tilde A_{e',T}>(\delta L_{e'})^2$);
   * (d) no tetrahedron $T$ incident to $e$ has a vertex or a face on the box surface.

   Every other tetrahedral edge is placed in $C$. Across the four meshes in `results_mixed.json`, the counts (`n_tets_non_well_centred` and `n_tets_touching_box` out of `n_tets`) are: $2669/3676$ non-well-centred and $3236/3676$ touching the box on key `test_delaunay`; $2094/3496$ and $2777/3496$ on key `cavity_N16`; $10\,463/23\,756$ and $12\,930/23\,756$ on key `N=10`; and $18\,972/50\,307$ and $23\,478/50\,307$ on key `N=16`.

2. **Voronoi diagonal Hodge $\mathbf D_V$ on $L$ and positivity proof.** In `SimplexMesh._dual_3d`, the perpendicular bisector plane of edge $e$ contains the edge midpoint $\mathbf m_e$, the circumcentres $\mathbf c_{f_1},\mathbf c_{f_2}$ of the two faces $f_1,f_2\subset T$ sharing $e$, and the tetrahedron circumcentre $\mathbf c_T$. Inside that plane, the segment $\mathbf m_e\to\mathbf c_{f_i}$ lies in face $f_i$ (orthogonal to $e$, with signed length $s_{e,f_i}=(\mathbf c_{f_i}-\mathbf m_e)\cdot\hat{\mathbf w}_{e,f_i}$ along the inward in-face normal $\hat{\mathbf w}_{e,f_i}$) and the segment $\mathbf c_{f_i}\to\mathbf c_T$ is normal to $f_i$ (with signed length $s_{f_i,T}=(\mathbf c_T-\mathbf c_{f_i})\cdot\hat{\mathbf n}_{f_i,T}$ along the inward face normal $\hat{\mathbf n}_{f_i,T}$). The two right triangles $(\mathbf m_e,\mathbf c_{f_1},\mathbf c_T)$ and $(\mathbf m_e,\mathbf c_{f_2},\mathbf c_T)$ have right angles at $\mathbf c_{f_i}$, giving the per-tetrahedron Voronoi dual face area (`cell_dual_area`)
   ```math
   \tilde A_{e,T}=\tfrac12\big(s_{e,f_1}\,s_{f_1,T}+s_{e,f_2}\,s_{f_2,T}\big).
   ```
   Because $\mathbf c_f$ lies in the plane of $f$, $s_{f,T}=(3V_T/A_f)\lambda_f(\mathbf c_T)$ is proportional to the barycentric coordinate $\lambda_f(\mathbf c_T)$ of $\mathbf c_T$ opposite face $f$; thus $\mathbf c_T$ lies strictly inside $T$ iff $s_{f,T}>0$ on all four faces. Likewise, $s_{e,f}=(2A_f/L_e)\lambda_{e,f}(\mathbf c_f)>0$ iff $\mathbf c_f$ lies strictly inside triangle $f$ (all four faces acute). When both $\mathbf c_T$ and every $\mathbf c_f$ lie strictly inside their simplices, both right-triangle legs $s_{e,f_i}>0$ and $s_{f_i,T}>0$ are positive, so each product $\tfrac12 s_{e,f_i}s_{f_i,T}>0$ and $\tilde A_{e,T}>0$. (In 3D, $s_{f,T}>0$ alone allows obtuse faces with $s_{e,f}<0$ and $\tilde A_{e,T}\le 0$, so `well_centred_tets` enforces both $s_{f,T}>\delta h_T$ and $\tilde A_{e,T}>(\delta L_e)^2$ with relative `margin` $\delta>0$.) For any edge $e\in L$, every incident tetrahedron $T\ni e$ is well-centred and live ($\varepsilon_T>0,\,\mathrm{live}_T=1,\,\tilde A_{e,T}>(\delta L_e)^2>0$), so the Voronoi diagonal entry
   ```math
   D_V[e]=\frac{\varepsilon_0}{L_e}\sum_{T\ni e}\varepsilon_T\,\mathrm{live}_T\,\tilde A_{e,T}>0
   ```
   is a sum of strictly positive terms divided by $L_e>0$.

3. **Mixed mass matrix, SPD proof, and invariance of the energy and stability proofs.** Up to a permutation grouping $L$ before $C$, `mixed_tet_mass` replaces $\mathbf M_1$ by
   ```math
   \mathbf M_\text{mixed}:=\mathrm{blockdiag}\big(\mathbf D_V|_L,\,\mathbf M_1|_C\big)=\mathbf I_C\mathbf M_1\mathbf I_C+\mathrm{diag}(\mathbf D_V|_L),
   ```
   dropping the $L$–$C$ and $L$ off-diagonal couplings of $\mathbf M_1$, and assembles
   ```math
   \mathbf M=\mathbf P^T\,\mathrm{blockdiag}\big(\mathrm{diag}(\mathbf m_\text{cart}),\,\mathbf M_\text{mixed}\big)\,\mathbf P.
   ```
   * **SPD proof.** $\mathbf D_V|_L$ is diagonal with strictly positive entries ($D_V[e]>0$), hence SPD. $\mathbf M_1|_C$ is a principal submatrix of $\mathbf M_1$ (which is SPD on the live tetrahedral edges), and every principal submatrix of an SPD matrix is SPD ($\mathbf x_C^T\mathbf M_1|_C\mathbf x_C=\tilde{\mathbf x}^T\mathbf M_1\tilde{\mathbf x}>0$ for all $\mathbf x_C\ne 0$). A block-diagonal matrix of SPD blocks is SPD, so $\mathbf M_\text{mixed}$ and $\mathbf M_\text{all}=\mathrm{blockdiag}(\mathrm{diag}(\mathbf m_\text{cart}),\,\mathbf M_\text{mixed})$ are SPD on the active/live edges. Finally, $\mathbf P=\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf I\\\mathbf T\end{bmatrix}$ has full column rank because its free-edge rows form the $n_\text{free}\times n_\text{free}$ identity $\mathbf I$, so $\mathbf x^T\mathbf M\mathbf x=(\mathbf P\mathbf x)^T\mathbf M_\text{all}(\mathbf P\mathbf x)>0$ for all $\mathbf x\ne 0$. *Test (`test_mixed_mass_spd`):* on the test Delaunay mesh (`results_mixed.json`, key `test_delaunay`: `n_tet_edges_lumped = 242` of `n_tet_edges = 5873`, `lump_fraction = 0.0412`), `dv > 0` on all lumped edges, both $\mathbf M_\text{mixed}$ and $\mathbf M$ are symmetric to machine precision, and their smallest Lanczos eigenvalues are strictly positive.
   * **Leapfrog energy, $\Delta t$ bound, and exact transpose.** The proofs in Section 3 (items 3 and 5) and Section 4 show that the leapfrog conserves
     ```math
     \mathcal E=\tfrac12\,\mathbf b^{n+1/2\,T}\mathbf W\mathbf b^{n+1/2}+\tfrac12\,\mathbf e^{nT}\mathbf M\mathbf e^{n+1}
     ```
     and is stable iff $\Delta t<2/\sqrt{\lambda_\max(\mathbf M^{-1}\mathbf K^T\mathbf W\mathbf K)}$. Those proofs depend only on $\mathbf W$ and $\mathbf M$ being SPD (or replaced by the SPD Chebyshev polynomial $p_k(\mathbf S)^{-1}$) and on $\mathbf K=\mathbf C\mathbf P$ and $\mathbf K^T$ being exact transposes (`(K.T - KT).nnz == 0`); the curl operators $\mathbf C,\mathbf K,\mathbf K^T$ and face Hodge $\mathbf W$ do not depend on the edge mass $\mathbf M$. *Tests (`test_dot_product_mixed`, `test_energy_drift_mixed`):* the curl transpose and full-step transpose hold to double-precision round-off for both LU and Chebyshev solves (whereas a non-symmetric mass perturbation breaks Chebyshev), and the leapfrog energy is conserved to double-precision round-off (whereas `mutate="asym"` drifts).

4. **Coupled block and Chebyshev degree.** `MassSolver` places in `S` only the rows of $\mathbf M$ with nonzero off-diagonal entries. Because $\mathbf P$ is the identity on $L$ and condition (d) excludes all tetrahedra touching the box surface, every edge in $L$ is uncoupled in $\mathbf M$ and inverted directly by `dinv`, so the coupled block loses at least $|L|$ rows (`n_tet_edges_lumped`), and the unscaled spectrum of the principal submatrix $\mathbf M_1|_C$ is interlaced by that of $\mathbf M_1$ (Cauchy's interlacing theorem). Because `MassSolver` applies Chebyshev iteration to the Jacobi-scaled block $\mathbf S=\mathbf D^{-1/2}\mathbf B\mathbf D^{-1/2}$ and the diagonal scaling changes when rows are removed, Cauchy interlacing of the unscaled principal submatrix is not sufficient to prove that the Jacobi-scaled condition number $\kappa=\lambda_\text{hi}/\lambda_\text{lo}$ cannot grow. In the measurements across all four cases in `results_mixed.json` (Batch job `w3hyb-m2`), the coupled block shrinks by exactly $|L|$ rows and the **Chebyshev degree `degree` is unchanged**:
   * `test_delaunay` (`results_mixed.json`, key `test_delaunay`): `coupled_block` shrinks from $2929$ to $2687$ (`n_tet_edges_lumped = 242` of `n_tet_edges = 5873`, `lump_fraction = 0.0412`); `degree = 162` for both `consistent` (`lam_lo = 0.04532`, `lam_hi = 3.374`) and `mixed` (`lam_lo = 0.04532`, `lam_hi = 3.374`).
   * `cavity_N16` (`results_mixed.json`, key `cavity_N16`): `coupled_block` shrinks from $3288$ to $2832$ (`n_tet_edges_lumped = 456` of `n_tet_edges = 5368`, `lump_fraction = 0.0849`); `degree = 86` for both `consistent` (`lam_lo = 0.1172`, `lam_hi = 2.517`) and `mixed` (`lam_lo = 0.1172`, `lam_hi = 2.515`).
   * `N=10` PEC sphere (`results_mixed.json`, key `N=10`): `coupled_block` shrinks from $24\,104$ to $16\,262$ (`n_tet_edges_lumped = 7842` of `n_tet_edges = 33\,179`, `lump_fraction = 0.2364`); `degree = 204` for both `consistent` (`lam_lo = 0.03000`, `lam_hi = 3.535`) and `mixed` (`lam_lo = 0.03000`, `lam_hi = 3.534`).
   * `N=16` PEC sphere (`results_mixed.json`, key `N=16`): `coupled_block` shrinks from $52\,064$ to $30\,025$ (`n_tet_edges_lumped = 22\,039` of `n_tet_edges = 68\,546`, `lump_fraction = 0.3215`); `degree = 502` for both `consistent` (`lam_lo = 0.005456`, `lam_hi = 3.907`) and `mixed` (`lam_lo = 0.005457`, `lam_hi = 3.907`).

   By Wathen's (1987) element-wise bound, the extreme eigenvalues `lam_lo`, `lam_hi` are set by the worst sliver tetrahedra, which are not well-centred and therefore stay in $C$, whereas the well-centred interior tetrahedra moved into $L$ sit in the interior of the spectrum.

5. **Limits, $\Delta t$ reduction, consistency loss, and mutations.**
   * **Shorter time step (`dt`).** Partial lumping shortens `dt` (and `dt_max`) in all four cases of `results_mixed.json` (and on the smoothed meshes in `results_smooth.json`):
     * key `test_delaunay`: `consistent.dt` $=1.72\times10^{-11}\text{ s}$ (`dt_max` $=1.81\times10^{-11}\text{ s}$) vs `mixed.dt` $=9.03\times10^{-12}\text{ s}$ (`dt_max` $=9.51\times10^{-12}\text{ s}$);
     * key `cavity_N16`: `consistent.dt` $=2.09\times10^{-11}\text{ s}$ (`dt_max` $=2.20\times10^{-11}\text{ s}$) vs `mixed.dt` $=1.81\times10^{-11}\text{ s}$ (`dt_max` $=1.91\times10^{-11}\text{ s}$);
     * key `N=10`: `consistent.dt` $=3.19\times10^{-11}\text{ s}$ (`dt_max` $=3.36\times10^{-11}\text{ s}$) vs `mixed.dt` $=2.80\times10^{-11}\text{ s}$ (`dt_max` $=2.95\times10^{-11}\text{ s}$); `consistent_smoothed.dt` $=3.17\times10^{-11}\text{ s}$ vs `mixed_smoothed.dt` $=2.31\times10^{-11}\text{ s}$ (`results_smooth.json`);
     * key `N=16`: `consistent.dt` $=1.90\times10^{-11}\text{ s}$ ($1.897\times10^{-11}\text{ s}$, `dt_max` $=2.00\times10^{-11}\text{ s}$) vs `mixed.dt` $=1.107\times10^{-11}\text{ s}$ (`dt_max` $=1.166\times10^{-11}\text{ s}$); `consistent_smoothed.dt` $=1.86\times10^{-11}\text{ s}$ vs `mixed_smoothed.dt` $=1.07\times10^{-11}\text{ s}$ (`results_smooth.json`).

     Because the global step `dt` is the binding stability constraint, `mass="mixed"` only reduces time-domain cost if the per-step saving on the smaller `coupled_block` outweighs the `dt` loss; `studies/hybrid-cart-tet/run_mixed_study.py` measures this trade-off via `time_to_solution_s` (`steps = ceil(T_phys / dt)` times `s_per_step`; see Section "Equal-accuracy study (mixed mass and smoothing)" below).
   * **Internal $L/C$ consistency loss and empty $L$ on Kuhn meshes.** Dropping the off-diagonal $\mathbf M_1$ couplings between $L$ and $C$ (and within $L$) introduces a first-order consistency error at the internal $L/C$ boundary where the mass switches from the lumped Voronoi Hodge $\mathbf D_V|_L$ to the consistent Whitney mass $\mathbf M_1|_C$. On `kuhn_box_mesh`, all circumcentres lie on the cube diagonals ($s_{f,T}=0$), so `well_centred_tets` is empty (`n_tet_edges_lumped = 0`) and `mass="mixed"` reduces to `mass="consistent"`. On the PEC cavity of `cavity_modes` at $N=16$ (`results_mixed.json`, key `cavity_N16`, `n_tet_edges_lumped = 456` of `n_tet_edges = 5368`, `lump_fraction = 0.0849`, `f_analytic_1 = 2.498270e8` Hz, `f_consistent_1 = 2.492068e8` Hz, `f_mixed_1 = 2.497163e8` Hz), the lowest resonance shifts by $|f_\text{mixed}-f_\text{consistent}|=5.10\times10^5\text{ Hz}$ (`diff_mixed_consistent`), which satisfies the acceptance criterion $|f_\text{mixed}-f_\text{consistent}|\le|f_\text{consistent}-f_\text{analytic}|=6.20\times10^5\text{ Hz}$ (`diff_consistent_analytic`; `ratio = 0.822 <= 1`, `test_cavity_eigenfrequency_mixed`).
   * **Mutations.** Lumping all tetrahedral edges without the well-centredness filter (`mutate="lump_all"`, `test_lump_all_mutation`) gives non-positive Voronoi entries ($\min D_V\le0$ and $\min\mathrm{diag}(\mathbf M)\le0$ on both the Kuhn and test Delaunay meshes, raising `RuntimeError` in `MassSolver` on Kuhn). Omitting only the `well_centred_tets` filter in `mixed_partition` (`mutate="ignore_wellcentred"`, `test_delaunay_wellcentred_mutation`) on the test Delaunay mesh lumps additional non-well-centred interior edges beyond the `n_tet_edges_lumped = 242` well-centred ones (`results_mixed.json`, key `test_delaunay`); its accuracy effect is not measured, because `HybridFIT3D` does not route `mutate="ignore_wellcentred"`.

#### 5. Meshing the patch

* **`kuhn_box_mesh`.** Freudenthal/Kuhn split of every (sub)cube into six tetrahedra around the main diagonal (Freudenthal 1942). It is conforming, deterministic and well shaped, and each boundary square gets one diagonal. Used by the fast tests.
* **`delaunay_box_mesh`.** Delaunay mesh of a box with an optional spherical hole (PEC) or tagged inclusion. The interior nodes form a body-centred cubic lattice, ODT-smoothed (Chen & Xu 2004) with the box nodes fixed and the sphere nodes projected back to the sphere. The sphere surface is recovered by removing interior nodes from the diametral balls of its hull triangles, as in `ball_mesh`. The box surface needs care, because a Delaunay mesh of a regular surface lattice is degenerate. The surface nodes are therefore:
  * the lattice nodes;
  * the midpoints of the surface lattice edges. Each half-edge is then a Gabriel edge: its diametral circle of radius $h/4$ is empty, since the nearest other node is at least $0.35h$ away. It therefore belongs to the face's 2D Delaunay triangulation, which the boundary of the 3D Delaunay mesh reproduces, so no triangle can cross a Cartesian edge;
  * one node near the centre of every square, jittered by up to 15 %, which breaks the cocircular degeneracies inside the squares.

  Lattice nodes on two faces next to a box edge can still form exactly cospherical planar quadrilaterals. Delaunay then emits zero-volume tetrahedra, and dropping them would leave a non-conforming seam. `_resolve_flat` removes each one by a 2-2 flip of the neighbour pair on one side of the quad, then raises if any degenerate cell remains. `box_surface_check` verifies that the triangles cover the box exactly and that each lies inside one square. Tested over 6 seeds × 4 configurations; the worst normalised volume is $10^{-3}$–$3\times10^{-2}$ (slivers).
* **`smooth_patch` (`wakis/hybrid/patch_quality.py`).** Opt-in fixed-topology post-smoothing of interior tetrahedral nodes keeping all box-surface, PEC-sphere/hole, and material-interface nodes fixed (`fixed_patch_nodes`) and all tetrahedron volumes strictly positive; see subsection "Patch mesh smoothing (`smooth_patch`)" above.

#### 6. Consistency and the patch test

Both sides reproduce constant fields and fields of constant curl exactly, and so does the trace:

* hexahedral Nédélec reproduces $\mathbf E=\mathbf a+\mathbf b\times\mathbf r$ per cell;
* $\mathbf P$ integrates it exactly along every hanging edge;
* Whitney forms contain $\mathbf a+\mathbf b\times\mathbf r$.

The interface therefore passes the lowest-order patch test. The remaining mismatch is that the tetrahedral tangential field on a square is the Whitney interpolant of a bilinear field, a local $O(h)$ difference with zero circulation on every triangle. In the measurements below it contributes at second order or better.

#### Equal-accuracy study (mixed mass and smoothing)

Script: `studies/hybrid-cart-tet/run_mixed_study.py` (invoked via `python studies/hybrid-cart-tet/run_mixed_study.py --ns 10 16 > studies/hybrid-cart-tet/results_mixed_study.json`; plotted by `studies/hybrid-cart-tet/plot_mixed_study.py` into `studies/hybrid-cart-tet/figures/fig_hybrid_mixed_tts_vs_error.png` and `studies/hybrid-cart-tet/figures/fig_hybrid_mixed_cost_per_unknown_step.png`).

* **Protocol.**
  1. **Frequency-domain far-zone accuracy (`pec_sphere_scattering`).** For each $N \in \{10, 16\}$ ($h = 1/N\text{ m}$, wavelength $\lambda = 1.0\text{ m}$, $ka = 2$, $a = 1/\pi\text{ m}$, `gap = 0.2`, `T = 0.5`) and each variant — `consistent` (`method="hybrid", mass="consistent", smooth=False`), `mixed` (`method="hybrid", mass="mixed", smooth=False`), `mixed_smoothed` (`method="hybrid", mass="mixed", smooth=True`), plus baselines `staircase` (`method="staircase"`) and `tet` (`method="tet"`) — `pec_sphere_scattering` solves the semi-discrete scattered-field system and evaluates `far_zone_error` (`err_far`). As stated in JSON key `far_zone_norm`, `err_far` is the relative $L_2$ norm $\|\mathbf e_\text{num} - \mathbf e_\text{ref}\|_2 / \|\mathbf e_\text{ref}\|_2$ of scattered edge voltages against the analytical PEC-sphere Mie series (`wakis.hybrid.mie_pec`) over all free Cartesian edges in the cubic shell $B + 0.25h < \|\mathbf x\|_\infty < B + \text{gap} - 0.25h$ spanning all $4\pi$ solid angles ($\theta \in [0, \pi]$, $\phi \in [0, 2\pi)$).
  2. **Time-domain step cost and unknown count.** On the time-domain PEC-box sphere geometry of part D ($v = h\,\mathrm{arange}(-n_h, n_h+1)$, $n_b = \lceil (a + \max(0.12, 2.2h))/h \rceil$, $n_h = n_b + 3$, `mass_solver="chebyshev"`, one CPU core with `OMP_NUM_THREADS = OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS = MKL_NUM_THREADS = 1`), each variant is stepped for $200$ calls of `one_step` (`s.run(200)`) after `msolver` assembly.   3. **Fixed physical duration $T_\text{phys}$ and time-to-solution.** Derived from the wavelength $\lambda = 1.0\text{ m}$ at $ka = 2$, one optical period of the source is $T_\text{phys} = \lambda / c_0 = 1.0 / c_0 \approx 3.33564\times 10^{-9}\text{ s}$ (JSON key `T_phys`). For each $(N, \text{variant})$, `steps = ceil(T_phys / dt)` and `time_to_solution_s = steps * s_per_step`.

As stated in JSON key `cart_unknowns_definition`, `cart_n_unknowns = cart_n_free_edges + cart_n_faces`, where `cart_n_free_edges` $= 3(2n_h)(2n_h - 1)^2$ is the count of active primary electric edges off the PEC outer walls (`ieps > 0`) and `cart_n_faces` $= 3(2n_h)^2(2n_h + 1)$ is the count of active primary magnetic faces (`imu > 0` and `grid.iA > 0`) on the $(2n_h)^3$ grid (`cart_n_padded = 6 * Nx * Ny * Nz` is the padded 3D `Field` array length).
  5. **Conformal `SolverFIT3D` option (`cart_conformal: null`).** `run_mixed_study.py` sets `cart_conformal: null` and records `cart_conformal_reason`: `SolverFIT3D` with `GridFIT3D(..., conformal="pec")` cannot execute the frequency-domain PEC-sphere scattered-field benchmark `pec_sphere_scattering` because (i) `SolverFIT3D` is a time-domain-only solver without `solve_frequency` or the complex-coordinate-stretching absorber (`stretch_functions`), and (ii) `wakis.conformal.apply_conformal_materials` embeds the homogeneous PEC boundary condition ($E_\text{tan} = 0$) into shortened open edge lengths `Ds` and the stabilized inverse face-area matrix `iDa` $= \mathbf T^T \mathrm{diag}(1/\hat A)\mathbf T$ rather than exposing sphere-surface edges for inhomogeneous scattered-field Dirichlet excitation ($-\mathbf v_\text{inc}$).

#### 8. Limits & Failure Modes

* **Interface reflection floor (open).** The TE₁₀ reflection of an empty patch falls fast (order 3–4) to about $10^{-4}$ at 16–20 cells per guide width, then flattens: $0.9$–$1.1\times10^{-4}$ (Kuhn) and $2$–$3\times10^{-4}$ (Delaunay) up to 40 cells, with observed order $\lesssim0.6$. The cause is not identified.
  * A row-wise truncation check, with exact TE₁₀ edge voltages inserted, shows $O(1)$ relative residuals: on the interface Cartesian rows 0.14 (Kuhn) and 0.23–0.30 (Delaunay), constant in $h$, and on deep tetrahedral rows as well. Whitney Galerkin rows converge through Galerkin orthogonality, not row-wise. The Cartesian bulk is $O(h^2)$.
  * Candidates: the non-conforming tangential trace (the Whitney interpolant of the bilinear hexahedral trace, equal circulations but a pointwise $O(1)$ basis-function jump), and the meeting of a lumped-quadrature (FIT) side with a consistent-mass Galerkin side.
  * Remedies to test: a pyramid transition layer (Rylander & Bondeson 2000), which is $H(\mathrm{curl})$-conforming; a mortar projection instead of the nodal trace; a lumped (Voronoi) tetrahedral mass on well-centred meshes.
  * For the scattering accuracy reported here (≥ 1 %), the floor is not the limiting error. Applications that need interface reflections below $10^{-4}$ should not rely on milestone 1.
* **Time step, cost, and two-rate stepping (open).** Each step needs a solve with the coupled mass block (`coupled_block` rows in `MassSolver`). On the unsmoothed meshes (`results_mixed.json`, Batch job `w3hyb-m2`), the Chebyshev `degree` of the `consistent` block is $162$ on `test_delaunay` (`coupled_block = 2929`), $86$ on `cavity_N16` (`coupled_block = 3288`), $204$ on `N=10` (`coupled_block = 24104`), and $502$ on `N=16` (`coupled_block = 52064`). Interior node smoothing (`smooth_patch`, `results_smooth.json`, Batch job `w3hyb-m3`, and `results_smooth_cart.json`, Batch job `w3hyb-m4`) raises `worst_volume_before` $\to$ `worst_volume_after` ($0.00120\to0.0293$ on `N=16`, $0.00605\to0.0114$ on `N=10`, $0.0136\to0.0238$ on `test_delaunay`, $0.0283\to0.0283$ on `cavity_N16`) and cuts the `consistent_smoothed` Chebyshev `degree` to $83$ on `N=16`, $124$ on `N=10`, $104$ on `test_delaunay`, and $85$ on `cavity_N16` (`lam_hi_before` $\to$ `lam_hi_after`: $3.907\to2.440$ on `N=16`, $3.535\to2.580$ on `N=10`, $3.374\to2.451$ on `test_delaunay`, $2.517\to2.330$ on `cavity_N16`). Yet smoothing leaves `dt` essentially unchanged (`consistent.dt` $\to$ `consistent_smoothed.dt`: $3.19\times10^{-11}\text{ s}\to3.17\times10^{-11}\text{ s}$ on `N=10`, $1.90\times10^{-11}\text{ s}\to1.86\times10^{-11}\text{ s}$ on `N=16`, $1.72\times10^{-11}\text{ s}\to1.71\times10^{-11}\text{ s}$ on `test_delaunay`, $2.09\times10^{-11}\text{ s}\to2.07\times10^{-11}\text{ s}$ on `cavity_N16`), while partial lumping (`mass="mixed"`) shortens `dt` in every measured case (`mixed.dt`: $2.80\times10^{-11}\text{ s}$ on `N=10`, $1.107\times10^{-11}\text{ s}$ on `N=16`, $9.03\times10^{-12}\text{ s}$ on `test_delaunay`, $1.81\times10^{-11}\text{ s}$ on `cavity_N16`; `mixed_smoothed.dt`: $2.31\times10^{-11}\text{ s}$ on `N=10`, $1.07\times10^{-11}\text{ s}$ on `N=16`, $1.71\times10^{-11}\text{ s}$ on `test_delaunay`, $2.07\times10^{-11}\text{ s}$ on `cavity_N16`). Hence the global tetrahedral step `dt` is set by something other than the mass conditioning; what sets `dt` is an open question. To compare `dt` against the pure-Cartesian step at the same grid spacing $h=1/N$, `studies/hybrid-cart-tet/measure_mixed.py` builds `HybridFIT3D(xn, yn, zn)` on the same node lines with no box (`dt_cartesian_same_h` in `results_smooth_cart.json`: $1.84\times10^{-10}\text{ s}$ at `N=10`, $1.15\times10^{-10}\text{ s}$ at `N=16`) and reports `dt_consistent_over_cartesian` ($0.174$ at `N=10`, $0.165$ at `N=16`) and `dt_mixed_over_cartesian` ($0.153$ at `N=10`, $0.0966$ at `N=16`), while `studies/hybrid-cart-tet/run_mixed_study.py` benchmarks `SolverFIT3D` on the same node lines (`cart_cost_per_unknown_step_s`, `cart_s_per_step`, `cart_n_unknowns`) and records `cart_conformal: null` because `SolverFIT3D(..., conformal="pec")` lacks frequency-domain complex-stretching solves and inhomogeneous scattered-field Dirichlet sphere edges (`cart_conformal_reason`). Finally, a two-rate (local time-stepping) step is not built: [`wakis/unstructured/lts.py`](../../wakis/unstructured/lts.py) derives its `modified_energy` conservation and exact transpose `linear_step_T` (`apply_T`) only for a second-order scalar system $\mathbf M\mathbf y''=-\mathbf K\mathbf y$ with a positive diagonal node mass $\mathbf M$ (so that $\mathbf M\mathbf A r(\mathbf X)=\mathbf K r(\mathbf X)$ is symmetric), whereas a two-rate step for `HybridFIT3D` needs an exact-transpose and modified-energy proof for a sub-cycled 3D edge-flux patch coupled through $\mathbf P$ and the non-diagonal mass block $\mathbf M$ that this wave has not derived.
* **Geometry of the patch.** There is one axis-aligned box of whole Cartesian cells, at least one cell from every outer wall. Patches touching walls or symmetry planes, several patches, and nesting inside `wakis.subgrid3d` patches are not implemented. The patch surface carries no finer tangential field than the Cartesian squares: finer triangles on the box surface are slaved to the bilinear trace.
* **Mesher.** `delaunay_box_mesh` handles a box with one sphere (hole or tagged inclusion). Other shapes need an external mesh whose box-surface triangles each lie inside one Cartesian square; `box_surface_check` enforces this. gmsh is GPL and may be used only as an external tool. Delaunay degeneracies are resolved by flips, and any remaining degenerate cell raises. Mesh quality is not guaranteed.
* **Materials and boundaries.** The tetrahedra may be vacuum, isotropic $\varepsilon,\mu$ per tag, or PEC holes; lossy and dispersive tetrahedra raise. The Cartesian cells use per-cell $\varepsilon,\mu$ and PEC (staircase). There is no time-domain absorber (no CPML) in the hybrid. Absorbers exist only as complex stretching in the frequency domain, in the Cartesian frame. There are no TF/SF sources, ports or near-to-far transforms.
* **Frequency-domain solves.** These are direct (SuperLU with a geometric nested-dissection order, no pivoting, a residual check and two refinement steps). Memory grows like the 3D fill: 36 GB for 565k unknowns ($N=16$, $h_t=h/2$). Larger problems need an iterative solver with a good preconditioner.
* **Not covered by the energy proof.** Any non-symmetric coupling: the `asym` mutation drifts. A Chebyshev tolerance $\ge1$ would make the polynomial lose positivity; the code uses $\le10^{-2}$ in tests. Lumping non-well-centred tetrahedral edges (`mutate="lump_all"`) destroys positive definiteness of $\mathbf M$.
* **Partial lumping (`mass="mixed"`).** Dropping the $L$–$C$ mass coupling at the internal boundary between lumped well-centred tetrahedra and consistent tetrahedra is a first-order consistency loss (`diff_mixed_consistent = 5.10e5` Hz vs `diff_consistent_analytic = 6.20e5` Hz, `ratio = 0.822` on the $N=16$ Delaunay cavity; `results_mixed.json`, key `cavity_N16`). Because the worst slivers are non-well-centred and stay in $C$, `mass="mixed"` shrinks `coupled_block` ($2929\to2687$ rows on `test_delaunay`, $3288\to2832$ on `cavity_N16`, $24\,104\to16\,262$ on `N=10`, $52\,064\to30\,025$ on `N=16`) while leaving `degree` unchanged ($162$, $86$, $204$, $502$; `results_mixed.json`). Moreover, lumping shortens `dt` in every measured case (`mixed.dt` vs `consistent.dt` in `results_mixed.json`: $9.03\times10^{-12}\text{ s}$ vs $1.72\times10^{-11}\text{ s}$ on `test_delaunay`, $1.81\times10^{-11}\text{ s}$ vs $2.09\times10^{-11}\text{ s}$ on `cavity_N16`, $2.80\times10^{-11}\text{ s}$ vs $3.19\times10^{-11}\text{ s}$ on `N=10`, and $1.107\times10^{-11}\text{ s}$ vs $1.90\times10^{-11}\text{ s}$ on `N=16`; `mixed_smoothed.dt` vs `consistent_smoothed.dt` in `results_smooth.json`: $2.31\times10^{-11}\text{ s}$ vs $3.17\times10^{-11}\text{ s}$ on `N=10`, $1.07\times10^{-11}\text{ s}$ vs $1.86\times10^{-11}\text{ s}$ on `N=16`). Because the global step is the binding constraint, `mass="mixed"` only helps if the `coupled_block` cost saving outweighs the `dt` loss; `studies/hybrid-cart-tet/run_mixed_study.py` evaluates `time_to_solution_s` alongside `far_zone_error` on the PEC sphere (see Section "Equal-accuracy study (mixed mass and smoothing)" above).
* **Performance and parallelism.** CPU only, global CSR, one core. There is no GPU, MPI or adjoint driver, beyond the exact `linear_step_T`.
