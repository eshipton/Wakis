# `wakis.subgrid` Toolkit Compatibility Audit

This document records the feature-by-feature compatibility audit between the 2D quadtree subgridding solver (`wakis.subgrid`: `QuadMesh`, `SubgridFIT2D`, `geometry.py`, `ports.py`, `monitors.py`, `pic.py`, `emitters.py`, `inverse.py`) and the Cartesian `SolverFIT3D` toolkit, along with the adapters added on `feature/subgrid-kit`, verification benchmarks in `tests/test_054_subgrid_compat.py`, and a ranked gap roadmap for the tutorial tracks.

## 2. Deep-Dive Notes on Key Physics & Adapters

### 2.1 Tutorial 4.3 Dispersive Metal Benchmark & Skin-Depth Refinement (`test_03`)
In `tutorials/04_radiation_and_antennas/04-03_dipole_near_mirror/`, a horizontal electric dipole (`Ex`, TE mode) sits at $d = 60\text{ nm} = 0.1\lambda_0$ ($\lambda_0 = 600\text{ nm}$) above a Drude silver mirror ($\varepsilon(\omega_0) \approx -6.0 - 0.8i$, skin depth $\delta = \frac{c}{\omega_0 \operatorname{Im}\sqrt{\varepsilon}} = 38.93\text{ nm}$). Coupling into the surface-plasmon polariton (SPP) pole enhances the radiative/total decay rate to $\Gamma / \Gamma_0 = 1.8427$ (computed via the exact 2D Sommerfeld / Chance-Prock-Silbey contour integral in `sommerfeld_2d_dipole_above_mirror`, vs $0.7926$ for an $E_z$ dipole and $0.5302$ for an $E_x$ dipole above a PEC mirror).

Using `refine_skin_depth(coarse_cell=30e-9, interface_box=..., material=metal, frequency=f0, cells_per_skin_depth=...)`:
- **Level 0 ($0.6\text{ cells}/\delta$, $\Delta x = 30\text{ nm}$)**: $4,769$ unknowns (`n_dof + n_edges`), $\Gamma/\Gamma_0 = 2.1183$, Purcell error **$14.96\%$**.
- **Level 1 ($1.3\text{ cells}/\delta$, $\Delta x = 15\text{ nm}$)**: $5,969$ unknowns, $\Gamma/\Gamma_0 = 1.9043$, Purcell error **$3.34\%$**.
- **Level 2 ($2.6\text{ cells}/\delta$, $\Delta x = 7.5\text{ nm}$)**: $11,113$ unknowns, $\Gamma/\Gamma_0 = 1.8394$, Purcell error **$0.18\%$** (vs $73,728$ unknowns on a uniform $7.5\text{ nm}$ grid — **$6.63\times$ fewer unknowns**, **$26.5\times$ fewer space-time updates** with `lts=4`).

Unlike a tensor-product graded Cartesian mesh (which forces thin $\Delta y = 7.5\text{ nm}$ strips across the entire $x$-extent of the domain and requires the global timestep $\Delta t$ to shrink everywhere), `QuadMesh` confines the $7.5\text{ nm}$ cells to the immediate vicinity of the dipole and metal interface and pairs with local time stepping (`lts=4`).

### 2.2 Subgrid Material Averaging: Strip vs Kottke Tensor Hodge (`test_08`)
- **How `SubgridFIT2D` averages materials by default (`averaging="strip"`)**:
  - In **TM mode** (`u = Ez` on nodes), `D` integrates $\varepsilon$ over the four node quadrants ($n_{\mathrm{sub}} \times n_{\mathrm{sub}}$ sub-grid quadrature). Because $\mathbf{E} = E_z \hat{z}$ is purely tangential to every 2D interface, scalar area-weighted averaging is already second-order accurate.
  - In **TE mode** (`u = Hz` on nodes, `f` = $\int \mathbf{E}\cdot d\mathbf{l}^*$ on dual edges), `averaging="strip"` divides the dual edge strip of width $L^*$ into $n_{\mathrm{sub}}$ columns along $L$, integrates $\varepsilon^{-1}$ in series across $L^*$ (`Rk`), and averages the inverse in parallel along $L$. This is exact for axis-aligned interfaces, **diagonal** in `W`, and does not couple orthogonal electric field components $(E_x, E_y)$ at tilted or curved boundaries.
- **Kottke Anisotropic Tensor Hodge (`averaging="kottke"`)**:
  - For each cut cell quadrant $q \in \{0, 1, 2, 3\}$ around an interior node, we compute the interface unit normal $\hat{n} = (n_x, n_y)$ from the sub-pixel permittivity gradient, the harmonic average $\eta_\perp = \langle \varepsilon^{-1}\rangle / \varepsilon_0$, and the arithmetic inverse $\eta_\parallel = (\varepsilon_0 \langle \varepsilon\rangle)^{-1}$, forming the symmetric $2\times 2$ inverse permittivity tensor:
    $$\boldsymbol{\eta}_q = \begin{pmatrix} \eta_\parallel n_y^2 + \eta_\perp n_x^2 & (\eta_\perp - \eta_\parallel) n_x n_y \\ (\eta_\perp - \eta_\parallel) n_x n_y & \eta_\parallel n_x^2 + \eta_\perp n_y^2 \end{pmatrix}.$$
  - We retain the exact full-dual-edge series-parallel strip diagonal `self.W` and assemble the symmetric off-diagonal coupling matrix `self.W_off` between the incident $x$-edge and $y$-edge of each cut quadrant with weight $w_{xy} = -\frac{A_q}{L^*_{e_x} L^*_{e_y}} \eta_{xy,q}$ (accounting for $f_x = -D_y L^*_x$, $f_y = +D_x L^*_y$).
  - Because `self.W + self.W_off` is real symmetric and diagonally dominant for all passive $\varepsilon > 0$, the discrete leapfrog energy $\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{f}^H (\mathbf{W} + \mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{off}})\mathbf{f} + \frac{1}{2}\mathbf{u}_{\mathrm{prev}}^H \mathbf{D}\mathbf{u}$ remains **exact to machine precision** ($1.82 \times 10^{-16}$ peak relative drift over 50 steps) and `step_pml_T` remains its exact transpose ($1.99 \times 10^{-16}$ in TE, $1.55 \times 10^{-15}$ in TM).

### 2.3 Exact Adjoint & `wakis.inverse` Integration (`test_10`)
- `SubgridFIT2D.step_pml` and `SubgridFIT2D.step_pml_T` provide the exact forward and reverse-mode transpose step over the full state $(u, f, u_x, u_y)$ including hanging-node prolongation $P$, Berenger split-field PML, static conductivity $\sigma$, Kottke tensor Hodge `W_off`, local time stepping (`lts`), and soft sources `q`.
- `SubgridMaterialGrid` computes the exact analytical rectangle-rectangle overlap matrix `M_sub` between an `(nx, ny)` SIMP design grid and the quadtree leaf node quadrants (TM) or dual-edge cells (TE), including hanging-node prolongation `Pk`.
- `SubgridAdjointSimulation` implements `output_shapes()`, `forward(rho, record=True)`, and `vjp(G)` using the exact same cotangent convention ($G = \partial L / \partial \operatorname{Re} X + i\,\partial L / \partial \operatorname{Im} X$) as `wakis.inverse.AdjointSimulation`, so `wakis.inverse.jax_simulation(adj_sim)` works out of the box without modifying `wakis/inverse/jax_tools.py`.

### 2.4 PML Reflection vs Incidence Angle: Subgrid Berenger vs Cartesian CFS-CPML (`test_07`)
- **Bloch sign convention & launched wave verification**: `DFTMonitor` accumulates $F(f) = \int F(t) e^{-2\pi i f t}\,dt$ ($e^{+i\omega t}$ phasor convention, `docs/physicsguide.md`). In `SolverFIT3D._setup_bloch`, `bloch_phase[a] = exp(+i k_bloch[a] L)`, so passing `k_bloch=(0.0, -ky, 0.0)` sets `bloch_phase[1] = exp(-i ky Ly)` matching `amp_func = exp(-i ky Y)` and excites a single $+x, +y$ wave $e^{i(\omega_0 t - k_x x - k_y y)}$ at $+f_0 > 0$. On the Cartesian reference run at $(0^\circ, 30^\circ, 60^\circ)$, phase-gradient fits at $x_{\mathrm{mon}} = +0.15\lambda_0$ give:
  - $k_y = \mathbf{0.0000, \, 10.4792, \, 18.1506\text{ rad/m}}$ (matching $k_0\sin\theta = 0.0000, \, 10.4792, \, 18.1506\text{ rad/m}$ to $< 10^{-14}$).
  - $k_x = \mathbf{21.0383, \, 18.2048, \, 10.5723\text{ rad/m}}$ (matching the exact Yee dispersion $k_{x,\mathrm{disc}} = 21.0383, \, 18.2049, \, 10.5723\text{ rad/m}$ to $< 10^{-4}$ and $k_0\cos\theta = 20.9585, \, 18.1506, \, 10.4792\text{ rad/m}$ to $< 0.9\%$).
  - Normalized incident amplitude $|E_z(\theta)|/|E_z(0^\circ)| = \mathbf{1.0000, \, 1.1504, \, 1.9647}$ (matching the exact discrete sheet-source Green's function ratio $\sin(k_{x,\mathrm{disc}}(0)h)/\sin(k_{x,\mathrm{disc}}(\theta)h) = 1.0000, \, 1.1508, \, 1.9654$ to $0.03\%$ and continuum $1/\cos\theta = 1.0000, \, 1.1547, \, 2.0000$ to $< 1.8\%$).
- **Why the previous $-96.94\text{ dB}$ at $60^\circ$ was a Floquet cutoff-leakage artifact**: At fixed $k_y = k_0\sin\theta$, the 1D channel dispersion $k_x(f) = \frac{2\pi}{c}\sqrt{f^2 - f_c^2}$ has cutoff $f_c = f_0\sin\theta$ ($0.8660 f_0$ at $60^\circ$) where $k_x \to 0$ and $v_{g,x} \to 0$. A broadband pulse (`fwidth = 0.35 * f0`) has $f_0 - f_c(60^\circ) = 0.38\,\sigma_f$, exciting the non-decaying cutoff oscillation at $93\%$ of peak amplitude; truncating it at $T = t_{\mathrm{end}} + 3\lambda_0/c$ distorted the reference wave ($k_x = 10.0023\text{ rad/m}$, $-5.39\%$ error; relative amplitude $2.1239$, $+8.06\%$ error) and created an accidental destructive-interference dip at $-96.94\text{ dB}$ that degraded to $-53.5\text{ dB}$ at longer integration windows. Keeping $f_0 - f_c(\theta) \ge 5\,\sigma_f$ (`fwidth = {0: 0.25*f0, 30: 0.12*f0, 60: 0.025*f0}`) with causally isolated reference domains eliminates the cutoff wake.
- **Two-regime theory of CFS-CPML reflection vs angle (why $-80.54\text{ dB}$ at $60^\circ$ is physical while $-96.94\text{ dB}$ is impossible)**:
  1. *PEC-backing floor*: Since $\sigma_{\max} = -\frac{(m+1)\ln R_0}{2 L \eta_0}$ uses $R_0 = 10^{-8}$ as the normal-incidence amplitude reflection, continuous round-trip **power** reflection from the PEC wall at $x = L_{\mathrm{PML}}$ scales as $|R_0^{\cos\theta}|^2 = R_0^{2\cos\theta}$ ($-160\cos\theta\text{ dB}$: $-160\text{ dB}$ at $0^\circ$, $-138.6\text{ dB}$ at $30^\circ$, and **$-80.0\text{ dB}$** at $60^\circ$; exact 1D discrete PEC-wall contribution on `GridFIT3D`'s 9 active layers with $\alpha_{\max}=0.05$ is **$-81.58\text{ dB}$** at $60^\circ$ and $-58.05\text{ dB}$ at $70^\circ$). Thus $-96.94\text{ dB}$ was $15.4\text{ dB}$ below the PEC-backing floor.
  2. *Staggered-grid discretization reflection*: Eliminating $H_x, H_y$ yields the exact 1D discrete PML equation $\frac{E_{j+1}-E_j}{s_{h,j+1/2}} - \frac{E_j-E_{j-1}}{s_{h,j-1/2}} + q(\theta)^2 s_{e,j} E_j = 0$, which depends on $\theta$ exclusively through the normal electrical step size $q(\theta) = 2\sin(k_{x,\mathrm{disc}}(\theta)h/2) \approx k_0 h \cos\theta$. Because $\kappa_{\max} = 5$ stretches the grid spacing inside the CPML by up to $5\times$, reflection at $0^\circ\text{--}30^\circ$ is dominated by front-layer discretization mismatch ($R_{\mathrm{disc}} = -69.98\text{ dB}$ at $0^\circ$, $-74.30\text{ dB}$ at $30^\circ$, $-92.62\text{ dB}$ at $60^\circ$), which decreases with $\theta$ as the normal wavelength $\lambda_x = \lambda_0/\cos\theta$ becomes better resolved ($20 \to 40\text{ cells}/\lambda_x$). At $60^\circ$, the rising PEC-wall reflection ($-81.58\text{ dB}$) overtakes $R_{\mathrm{disc}}$ ($-92.62\text{ dB}$), giving a total reflection of **$-80.54\text{ dB}$**.
- **Converged reflection vs angle (10 cells, $\Delta = \lambda_0 / 20$)**:
  - **Subgrid Berenger PML** (`R0=1e-8`, `order=3`): **$-89.89\text{ dB}$** ($0^\circ$), **$-91.32\text{ dB}$** ($30^\circ$), **$-77.11\text{ dB}$** ($60^\circ$).
  - **Cartesian CFS-CPML** (`R0=1e-8`, `pml_exp=4`, `kappa_max=5`, `alpha_max=0.05`): **$-70.00\text{ dB}$** ($0^\circ$), **$-74.30\text{ dB}$** ($30^\circ$), **$-80.54\text{ dB}$** ($60^\circ$).

## 3. Ranked Gaps for the Tutorial Tracks

Ordered by impact across the four tutorial families (**PIC**, **Emitters**, **Metasurfaces**, **Vacuum Electronics**):

1. **[Rank 1 — Vacuum Electronics & RF Cavities] Conformal Small-Cell CFL Bypass (Dey-Mittra / ECT) & SIBC on Subgrid (`~2–3 days`)**
   - *Why it matters*: While `material="pec"` and static conductivity `sigma` now work with sub-pixel fraction weighting (`test_09`), in TE mode a curved PEC wall that clips a tiny sliver ($< 5\%$) of a cell currently zeros out the clipped area (`open_area < 0.05 * area`) to avoid $\Delta t \to 0$. True Dey-Mittra area/length borrowing or a dispersive Surface Impedance Boundary Condition (SIBC) for copper RF walls at GHz/THz frequencies would eliminate staircase artifacts on high-$Q$ curved cavities without shrinking $\Delta t$.
2. **[Rank 2 — Metasurfaces & Plasmonics Inverse Design] Dispersive ADE Adjoint & Checkpointing in `SubgridAdjointSimulation` (`~1.5 days`)**
   - *Why it matters*: `SubgridAdjointSimulation` currently differentiates dielectric (`eps_r`) and conductive (`sigma`) designs with PML and mode/DFT monitors (`test_10`). Adding the reverse-mode transpose of `SubgridFIT2D.groups` (Drude/Lorentz auxiliary polarization states `p, p_old`) and plugging in `wakis.inverse.memory.CheckpointRung` will enable topology optimization of plasmonic nano-antennas and long-time high-$Q$ metasurface resonators.
3. **[Rank 3 — Metasurfaces & Emitters] Total-Field / Scattered-Field (TF/SF) Closed Contour Injection (`~1 day`)**
   - *Why it matters*: `SubgridFIT2D.add_plane_wave` uses the volumetric scattered-field formulation (which is exact for bounded scatterers in a homogeneous background and now supports `add_bloch_plane_wave` for periodic gratings/slabs). A closed-contour Huygens TF/SF box on `SubgridFIT2D` would allow finite-beam or plane-wave injection onto scatterers sitting on multi-layer substrates where the background is piecewise homogeneous.
4. **[Rank 4 — PIC & Accelerator Wakefields] 2D Axisymmetric ($r\text{–}z$) Coordinate Weighting in `SubgridFIT2D` (`~2 days`)**
   - *Why it matters*: Vacuum electronics and accelerator wakefield tutorials frequently model cylindrically symmetric cavities and iris-loaded structures ($m = 0, 1$ azimuthal modes). Adding radial $2\pi r$ metric factors to `QuadMesh` node areas $D$ and edge lengths $W$ (with $r = 0$ axis regularity) would allow 2D subgridding around sharp cavity irises with full 3D axisymmetric accuracy.
5. **[Rank 5 — Large-Scale PIC & Metasurfaces] GPU (`cupy`) Acceleration for `SubgridFIT2D` (`~2–3 days`)**
   - *Why it matters*: Moving `K, KH, _BWx_p` to `cupyx.scipy.sparse.csr_matrix` and vectorizing the PML/ADE/PIC gather-deposit updates on GPU would speed up large subgrid meshes ($> 10^5$ unknowns or $> 10^5$ macro-particles).
6. **[Rank 6 — Full 3D Photonics / Emitters / Wakefields] 3D Octree Subgridding (`~3–4 weeks`)**
   - *Why it matters*: Needed when neither 2D planar nor 2D axisymmetric symmetry applies (e.g., 3D photonic crystal cavities, 3D waveguide bends with vertical radiation, or 3D off-axis wakefields).

## 4. 3D Octree Subgridding Sketch (`OctMesh` & `SubgridFIT3D`)

`wakis.subgrid` is **2D only** (`QuadMesh` and `SubgridFIT2D`). Extending Wakis's energy-conserving prolongation-based subgridding to 3D in `SolverFIT3D` requires the following five components:

1. **2:1-Balanced Octree Topology (`OctMesh`, ~1 week)**:
   - Replace the 2D 4-child quadtree (`(level, i, j)`) with a 3D 8-child octree (`(level, i, j, k)`) encoded by 64-bit 3D Morton keys, enforcing the 2:1 balance invariant across **faces (6)** and **edges (12)** so that any coarse cell shares a face or edge with cells at most one refinement level finer ($r = 2$ or $3$).
   - Extract unique primal **nodes**, primal **edges** ($e_x, e_y, e_z$), and primal **faces** ($f_x, f_y, f_z$), classifying each into *free* (active DOFs) and *hanging* (constrained DOFs on coarse-fine transition faces/edges).
2. **Commuting Edge & Face Prolongation Matrices $P_e, P_f$ (~1 week)**:
   - In 2D scalar/vector FIT (`SubgridFIT2D`), only nodes can hang because a hanging edge midpoint is a hanging node ($K = G P$).
   - In 3D vector FIT, **both edges and faces hang** on coarse-fine transition interfaces. Stability and absence of late-time spurious charges require the discrete curl to commute with prolongation:
     $$C_{\mathrm{fine}} P_e = P_f C_{\mathrm{coarse}}, \qquad K_e = P_f^T C_{\mathrm{fine}} P_e.$$
   - For a $2\times 2$ split coarse face:
     - Each coarse edge split into two fine sub-edges has prolongation weight $1/2$ along the split edge; the two interior fine edges crossing the face midpoint interpolate bilinearly from the two parallel coarse edges with weight $1/2$.
     - Each of the 4 fine sub-faces has prolongation weight $1/4$ from the parent coarse face.
     - With these weights, Stokes' theorem holds identically on every coarse and fine face ($C_{\mathrm{fine}} P_e = P_f C_{\mathrm{coarse}}$), guaranteeing $\nabla_h \cdot (\nabla_h \times \mathbf{E}) = 0$ and $\nabla_h \cdot \mathbf{B} = 0$ across all octree interfaces.
3. **Symmetric Positive-Definite Diagonal Hodges $D_\varepsilon, D_\mu^{-1}$ (~3 days)**:
   - Assemble fine-cell elemental diagonal electric and magnetic Hodges $\tilde{M}_\varepsilon, \tilde{M}_{\mu}^{-1}$ using sub-voxel quadrature + 3D Kottke tensor averaging (`wakis.tensor_averaging`), and project onto the free DOFs via:
     $$M_\varepsilon = P_e^T \tilde{M}_\varepsilon P_e, \qquad M_\mu = P_f^T \tilde{M}_\mu P_f,$$
     using lumped dual-volume quadrature at the hanging edges/faces so $M_\varepsilon$ and $M_\mu$ remain strictly diagonal (or block-diagonal on Kottke cut cells) and symmetric positive-definite.
4. **Energy-Conserving Leapfrog & Berenger/CFS-CPML (~4 days)**:
   - Advance free edge voltages $\mathbf{e} = \int \mathbf{E}\cdot d\mathbf{l}$ and free face fluxes $\mathbf{b} = \int \mathbf{B}\cdot d\mathbf{S}$ via the symmetric pair $(K_e, K_e^H)$:
     $$\mathbf{b}^{n+1/2} = \mathbf{b}^{n-1/2} - \Delta t\, K_e \mathbf{e}^n, \qquad \mathbf{e}^{n+1} = \mathbf{e}^n + \Delta t\, M_\varepsilon^{-1} K_e^H M_\mu^{-1} \mathbf{b}^{n+1/2},$$
     paired with `wakis.unstructured.lts` Chebyshev local time stepping on the fine octree levels so the coarse grid runs at the coarse CFL $\Delta t_{\mathrm{coarse}} = r^{\ell_{\max}} \Delta t_{\mathrm{fine}}$.
5. **Monitors, Ports, and Adjoint Compatibility (~3 days)**:
   - Reuse `wakis.mode_solver`, `DFTMonitor`, `FluxMonitor`, `Near2FarMonitor`, and `AdjointSimulation` by composing their Cartesian plane/box sampling operators with $P_e$ and $P_f$.
